# Reflection — Dear Fridge

Dear Fridge is designed to empower busy students, young people living alone, and anyone who wants to waste less food without turning everyday cooking into another demanding task. These users may have limited time, irregular routines, small kitchens, or little confidence in planning meals. The project imagines a future in which a connected fridge does not simply automate decisions. Instead, it helps people notice what they already have and make their own choices with more confidence.

This matters because food waste often begins with small moments of forgetting: an ingredient hidden at the back of a shelf, a leftover that is not used in time, or an unnecessary purchase made without checking the fridge. For students and people living alone, these small losses can also have a meaningful financial cost. Dear Fridge responds with gentle reminders and practical suggestions. It connects inventory, cooking inspiration, and shopping in one clear flow, while still leaving the user in control. The design does not pretend to buy food or cook automatically; it supports attention rather than replacing it.

The visual choices express this future as warm and approachable. Many smart-kitchen interfaces use cold colors, dense dashboards, and technical language. I chose watercolor food illustrations, cream paper textures, handwritten notes, soft sage green, and generous empty space so that the experience feels more like a personal kitchen diary. The realistic fridge view gives an immediate sense of where food is stored, while the category view makes scanning faster. The Recipe Book turns current inventory into possible meals and gives priority to ingredients that should be used soon. The Restock Basket continues the same system into shopping, suggesting only what is running low.

Together, these choices present technology as a quiet form of everyday care. The future imagined here is not about a kitchen that knows everything. It is about a kitchen that helps people remember, improvise, and waste a little less.
